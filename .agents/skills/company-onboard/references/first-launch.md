# First-launch experience

Read this reference when onboarding includes the first authenticated experience, a proof moment, or
a credible team handoff.

## Choose one concept

Synthesize the company, initial user, requested operation, and brand into one operating emphasis and
one memorable proof moment. The proof should leave a real result worth reviewing or showing to a
teammate: a saved recommendation with rationale, a safely previewed consequential action, a newly
clear relationship, or another outcome specific to the request. Generic CRUD is supporting
infrastructure unless it genuinely fulfills the operation.

Take creative ownership of company-owned surfaces, but do not invent facts, official marks, claims,
or shipped capabilities. Earn recognition through specificity and competence rather than themed
decoration.

## Design the reveal in launch order

The experience should create four reactions:

1. **Recognition:** the identity, language, and useful data feel unmistakably company-owned.
2. **Proof:** the requested operation and its primary action are understandable and work.
3. **Team pull:** the result persists and has an honest authorized review or handoff path.
4. **Momentum:** one valuable refinement or adjacent workflow is concrete and close.

Reveal this through the product rather than a welcome tour. Treat Company OS as the foundation, not
the required visible product name. Connect the first operation to a broader ambition without
presenting proposed automation as already delivered.

The authenticated home should answer: What does this know about us? What can I do now? What could we
build next? Center it on the few records, decisions, or statuses required by the operation, provide
one unmistakable primary action, and leave a persistent result another authorized person can
understand. Use public or synthetic starting data only when its provenance and non-authoritative
status are clear.

When collaboration matters, point membership changes to the deployment identity provider and
surface a natural governed handoff. Do not add a decorative Share button, bypass permissions, or
publish company data.

When automation matters, state what starts the work, which steps run without a person, where
approval is required, how failures surface, and what outcome is recorded. The presence of an agent
or schedule is not evidence of autonomy.

After the operation works, inspect the experience in launch order—gateway entry when observable,
first authenticated screen, primary workflow, team handoff, then expansion—and polish the largest
weakness. A beautiful entry followed by a generic home, or a polished screen with a non-working
action, is not a successful first launch.
